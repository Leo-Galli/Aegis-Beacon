# Phonetics

Spelling a callsign or a coordinate letter-by-letter over voice is where rescue communication goes wrong fastest: B, P, T, D, E all sound similar on a scratchy channel. The NATO phonetic alphabet exists to make every letter unambiguous.

## The alphabet

| A Alfa | B Bravo | C Charlie | D Delta | E Echo |
| F Foxtrot | G Golf | H Hotel | I India | J Juliett |
| K Kilo | L Lima | M Mike | N November | O Oscar |
| P Papa | Q Quebec | R Romeo | S Sierra | T Tango |
| U Uniform | V Victor | W Whiskey | X Xray | Y Yankee | Z Zulu |

## Why it matters here

- The beacon transmits identity in Morse: the receiving side decodes it as letters. When that side reports the identity by voice, phonetics make the letters survive the voice channel.
- A callsign like `IK2XYZ` spoken plainly sounds like several other callsigns; spelled `India Kilo Two Xray Yankee Zulu` it is unambiguous.
- Coordinates contain the letters N, E, W, S: `45.83 N` spoken as "November" instead of "en" removes the N/S confusion that has misdirected searches.

## The practice rule

When you give a callsign or coordinate letters by voice:

1. State the plain version first.
2. Spell it phonetically.
3. Have the receiver read it back phonetically.
