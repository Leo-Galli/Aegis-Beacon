# Amateur License Process

The amateur license converts the beacon from a limited ISM device into a full-capability radio. The process is bureaucratic but short, and this page is the map.

## The four steps

| Step | What happens | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Study | Learn the theory: bands, propagation, safety, operating practice | 2 - 6 weeks |
| 2. Exam | Pass the theory exam, online or at a local club | 1 day |
| 3. Apply | Submit the application to the national regulator | 1 - 4 weeks |
| 4. Receive | Get your callsign and certificate | Same day as step 3 in most countries |

## Study resources

Every country has official exam syllabi and question banks:

- Italy: the ARI and the ministry's question bank
- Switzerland: OFCOM's online exam and study guide
- UK: the RSGB Foundation materials
- US: the FCC question pools, free practice apps

The syllabus for the entry class covers exactly the material in this wiki's [RF Basics](rf-basics), [Propagation](propagation-and-range) and [Safety](rf-exposure-safety) pages: studying for the license and understanding the beacon reinforce each other.

## After the license

1. Program your callsign into the beacon's identity settings (see [Config Payload Format](config-payload-format)).
2. Confirm the beacon transmits the callsign on every burst (see [Callsign Identification](callsign-identification)).
3. Keep the certificate with you: you may be asked to show it, and the beacon's SEARCH mode lets you find other licensed operators.

## The cost

Entry exams cost roughly 20 - 100 euros depending on the country, with no recurring fee in most places. Compared with commercial emergency beacons, the license is the cheap part of the setup.
