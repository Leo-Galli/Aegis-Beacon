# Beacon Care Seasonal

The beacon is a tool, and tools need a maintenance cycle. This page is the season rhythm: what to do before the season, after it, and while the beacon waits in a drawer.

## The pre-season service

| Check | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Full bench test | The complete [Two Beacon Bench Test](two-beacon-bench-test) |
| Firmware review | Is the installed version current? (see [Release Process](release-process)) |
| Seals inspection | Gaskets, SMA O-rings, button membranes (see [Waterproofing](waterproofing-and-enclosure-sealing)) |
| Cell health | Capacity check: does it hold a charge? (see [Battery Troubleshooting](battery-troubleshooting)) |
| Config review | Callsign, channels, identity current for this season |

## The post-season inspection

After the last trip of the season:

1. Full clean and dry (see [Cleaning and Inspection](cleaning-and-inspection)).
2. Inspect connectors for corrosion (see [Water Damage Recovery](water-damage-recovery)).
3. Log anything that misbehaved (see [Beacon Log Template](beacon-log-template)).
4. Charge the cell to 60% and store.

## The storage rule

A stored cell at 100% ages fastest; at 0% it can die. The storage sweet spot is 40 - 60% charge, cool and dry (see [Battery Storage](battery-storage)). Every 3 months: check the storage voltage, top up to 60% if it fell.

## The drawer surprise

A beacon stored for six months is not the beacon you tested: seals harden, the cell self-discharges, and the config may be a season stale. The [Pack Check Routine](pack-check-routine) exists precisely because of this. Run it before every trip, including the first of the season.
