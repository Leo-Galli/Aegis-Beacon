# First Aid Kit

A first aid kit is the medical side of the same story the beacon tells: something can go wrong, and preparation is the treatment. This page is the mountain-specific kit list.

## The kit

| Item | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Bandages and sterile dressings | Bleeding control |
| Adhesive tape | Dressings, splints, blisters |
| Triangular bandage | Slings, immobilization |
| Blister plasters | The most-used item on the list |
| Antiseptic wipes | Wound cleaning |
| Pain relief | Ibuprofen, paracetamol |
| Antihistamine | Allergic reactions, insect bites |
| Thermal blanket | Hypothermia (see [Hypothermia Recognition](hypothermia-recognition)) |
| Small scissors, tweezers | Cutting, splinters, ticks |
| Tape and safety pins | Improvised everything |

## Skills, not items

The kit is worthless without the skills: how to apply a pressure dressing, how to improvise a splint, how to recognize shock. Take a first aid course aimed at outdoor or wilderness contexts; the kit earns its weight after the course, not before.

## The beacon connection

The kit and the beacon cooperate in the two emergency scenarios:

| Scenario | Kit role | Beacon role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Injury | Treat, stabilize, shelter | Transmit position and repeat |
| Missing / lost | Warmth, food, signaling | Transmit until found |

A beacon transmitting from a treated, sheltered subject is the outcome every rescue system is designed around.

## Kit care

Check the kit every season: expiry dates, used items, and the thermal blanket (one-time use). A kit that lived through a winter in a damp pack has a way of quietly losing its contents.
