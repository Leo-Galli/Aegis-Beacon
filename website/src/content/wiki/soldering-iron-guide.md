# Soldering Iron Guide

A good iron and a clean tip make the difference between a tidy board and a rework session. This page covers what to buy and how to use it.

## What to buy

- **Minimum**: a 25-40 W fixed-temperature iron. Works, but temperature control is manual (plug/unplug).
- **Recommended**: a temperature-controlled station (e.g. 936-style clones or the Hakko FX-888) with an adjustable range of 200-450 C.
- **Tip**: a conical or small chisel tip (1.0-1.6 mm) suits the module pads. A 2.4 mm chisel helps for ground pours.

## Temperatures

- **Lead solder (60/40)**: 300-330 C.
- **Lead-free (SAC305)**: 350-380 C.
- **Too hot** (400+): lifted pads and burned flux.
- **Too cold** (under 280): dull, spiky joints and cold solder.

## Technique for module pads

1. Tin the tip with a little fresh solder.
2. Touch the pad and the wire lead together with the tip for 1-2 seconds.
3. Feed solder at the junction, not onto the tip.
4. Remove the iron and let the joint cool without moving it.
5. A good joint is shiny (with lead), wets both surfaces, and forms a smooth fillet.

## Common defects

| Defect | Cause | Fix |
|--------|-------|-----|
| Dull, grainy joint | Cold solder or lead-free at low temp | Reheat with flux |
| Solder bridge | Too much solder between pads | Wick it away |
| Spike | Iron removed too slowly | Less solder, faster lift |
| Lifted pad | Too hot or too long | Lower temp, shorter dwell |

## Iron care

- Wipe the tip on a damp sponge or brass wool after every few joints.
- Never file a tip; a new tip is cheap.
- Keep the tip tinned when not in use to prevent oxidation.
- Unplug the station when you leave the bench.
