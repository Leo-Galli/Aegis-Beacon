# Winter Operations

## Overview

The beacon is designed for backcountry use, and winter is its hardest environment. Cold drains cells, gloves fight buttons, and snow eats signal. This page consolidates the winter-specific guidance.

## Battery Strategy

| Factor | Reality | Response |
|--------|---------|----------|
| Capacity at -20 C | 40-60% of nominal | Start at 100%, carry a spare |
| LiFePO4 | Rated to -30 C | Prefer for alpine deployments |
| Internal resistance | Rises as it cools | Voltage sags under TX load |

Keep the beacon in an inside pocket until needed. Body heat is the cheapest battery warmer.

## Snow and Signal

- 433 MHz attenuates ~3 dB/m in wet snow.
- A buried beacon at +30 dBm PA still overcomes ~1 m of burial.
- EMERGENCY mode is designed for exactly this case.

## Glove-Friendly Operation

| Control | Glove tip |
|---------|-----------|
| MODE short press | Practice the toggle with gloves on before the trip |
| MODE long press (2 s) | Count "one, two" deliberately |
| SEL (3 s for CONFIG) | Use the dashboard for complex changes at home |
| UP/DN | Not needed in the field if configured at home |

## Winter Handling

1. Do not charge a frozen cell - warm it first.
2. Keep the OLED facing away from direct sun glare; invert mode helps in bright snow light.
3. Snow on the SMA can detune the antenna; clear the connector.
4. After a day out, dry the case before storage to prevent corrosion.
