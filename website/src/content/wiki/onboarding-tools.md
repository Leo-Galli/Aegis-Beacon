# Tools You Need

You can build the beacon with a surprisingly small toolkit. Here is the minimum, the recommended, and the optional.

## Minimum

- **Soldering iron** (25-40 W or a temperature-controlled station). Any iron that heats fast and holds temperature works.
- **Solder** (0.7-1.0 mm 60/40 or lead-free rosin-core). Thin solder makes fine joints easier.
- **Wire cutters / side cutters** for trimming leads.
- **Wire strippers** (22-28 AWG range).
- **Small flathead or Phillips screwdriver** for the enclosure and terminals.
- **Helping hands** (optional but very useful) to hold wires while soldering.

## Recommended

- **Temperature-controlled soldering station** with a fine tip.
- **Flux pen** for stubborn joints (especially ground planes and the SMA connector).
- **Digital multimeter** for continuity checks, voltage readings, and the battery divider test.
- **Desoldering wick** for fixing mistakes.
- **Third hand with magnifying glass**.
- **Heat shrink tubing** (2-4 mm) for protecting solder joints.

## For flashing

- A **USB micro or USB-C cable** that carries data (many cheap cables are charge-only).
- A computer with the Arduino IDE or PlatformIO installed.

## For testing

- A **dummy load** (50 ohm) if you want to transmit on the bench without radiating.
- Optionally an RTL-SDR dongle to hear the beacon on another device.

## Nice to have

- A bench power supply (or a power bank) for powering the beacon while testing.
- An oscilloscope for RF and audio debugging.
- A frequency counter.

None of the nice-to-have items are required for a first build. Start with the minimum, and upgrade only when you hit a real limitation.
