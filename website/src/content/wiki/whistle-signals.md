# Whistle Signals

The whistle is the oldest radio in the kit: no batteries, no license, works in a storm. Its codes are standardized enough that any mountaineer anywhere will recognize them.

## The distress code

| Signal | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Six blasts, pause, repeat | Distress |
| Three blasts | Reply: "we hear you" |
| One long blast | Attention, not distress |

Six and three are the international mountain convention (three is also the SOS-derived count). Memorize both directions: you may be the one replying.

## Making it carry

- Blow full and steady: a whistle is louder when your cheeks are full and the air column is steady.
- Blast from a high spot; sound carries over terrain better from elevation.
- Cup the whistle or blow from a ridge to direct the sound.
- A metal pea whistle carries farther than a plastic one and survives cold better.

## Whistle vs beacon

| Situation | Tool |
| --- | --- |
| Searchers visibly nearby | Whistle |
| Searchers out of sight | Beacon |
| Night, storm, long range | Beacon |
| Confirm a visual sighting | Whistle (three blasts) |

The beacon transmits for hours; the whistle works for one encounter. Use the whistle when you know the window is open, and save the beacon for the long haul.

## The reply protocol

If you hear six blasts:

1. Reply with three blasts.
2. Note the direction.
3. Continue replying every minute so the caller can home in.
4. Keep the beacon transmitting throughout.
