# Winter Pack

Winter multiplies every beacon risk: batteries sag, seals stiffen, snow blocks antennas, and hypothermia shortens the response window. This page is the winter pack.

## The winter additions

| Item | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Two spare cells, inside pockets | Cold kills capacity (see [Cold Weather Batteries](cold-weather-batteries)) |
| Heated cell pouch or body placement | The cell must stay warm to deliver TX current |
| Glove-compatible antenna mount | Antennas snap in the cold |
| Signal mirror and whistle | Cold weather often means clear skies: daylight signaling works |
| Thermal blanket in the beacon pouch | The beacon pouch doubles as the survival kit (see [Emergency Kit](emergency-kit)) |

## The battery strategy

1. Cell A: in the beacon, insulated.
2. Cell B: inside jacket, body heat.
3. Swap at the first sign of TX sag, not at the first low reading (see [Battery Field Swap](battery-field-swap)).

## The snow antenna problem

Snow on the antenna adds loss and can detune it (see [Snow and Signal Behavior](snow-and-signal-behavior)). A quick wipe before an important burst helps; mounting the antenna clear of the pack's snow collection points helps more.

## The winter checks

- Seals: cold rubber seals stiffen; check the case seal closes fully (see [Waterproofing](waterproofing-and-enclosure-sealing)).
- The 45-minute rule: in deep cold, check the beacon's operation (a test burst) within 45 minutes of departure, not at the summit.
- The [Pack Check Routine](pack-check-routine) runs twice: at home and at the trailhead.
