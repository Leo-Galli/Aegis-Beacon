# Safety Quick Rules

The beacon is safe to build and use, and "safe" is a set of rules, not a feeling. This page is the ten non-negotiables; each one links to its full treatment.

## The battery rules

1. Protected cells only: a bare cell is a hazard (see [Protected vs Unprotected](protected-vs-unprotected)).
2. Never charge unattended at high current (see [TP4056 Charging Current](tp4056-charging-current)).
3. A swollen or hot cell gets recycled, immediately (see [Battery Troubleshooting](battery-troubleshooting)).

## The RF rules

4. Never connect a spectrum analyzer or sensitive receiver directly to the antenna port: use an attenuator (see [Spectrum Analyzers](spectrum-analyzers)).
5. Antennas and people: keep the radiating element away from eyes and skin during TX (see [RF Exposure Safety](rf-exposure-safety)).
6. During lightning: antenna down or the device off (see [Storm Operations](storm-operations)).

## The field rules

7. The beacon is one layer of the safety stack, never the whole stack (see [Hiking Safety](hiking-safety)).
8. The beacon does not replace an avalanche transceiver (see [Avalanche Safety Basics](avalanche-safety-basics)).
9. An emergency transmission is justified; a drill at emergency power is not (see [Emergency Use Legal](emergency-use-legal)).
10. A beacon that fails the bench test stays home (see [Bench Checklist](bench-checklist)).

## The one rule above all

When in doubt, power down and think. Every failure mode in this wiki is survivable; the ones that are not start with ignoring the warning signs.
