# Mountain Effects on Radio

Mountains are the beacon's home turf, and they are also its worst enemy. This page is the tactical manual for radio in relief.

## Ridge shadowing

A ridge between beacon and receiver blocks the direct path. The shadow can cost 20 - 40 dB, effectively ending the link. The signal does not fully disappear: diffraction over the ridge edge leaks some energy through, which is why a beacon "sometimes" works from behind a ridge.

## Knife-edge diffraction

When the path just clips a ridge top, the edge acts as a diffracting knife edge and the loss is surprisingly modest (10 - 15 dB). This is why a beacon directly below a summit may be heard from the other side while one deeper in a valley is not: the geometry of the grazing edge matters more than the distance.

## Valley trapping

A deep valley confines the signal; reflections off the walls add, but the direct path is gone. In a narrow valley the effective range can be a few hundred meters regardless of power. The classic fix is to climb out of the valley floor until the walls clear the path.

## The tactical playbook

| Situation | Move |
| --- | --- |
| Beacon in valley, you on the slope | Ascend until you see the beacon's ridge |
| Ridge between you and the beacon | Climb to the ridge crest; diffraction doubles |
| Search in a valley | Check the crest first, then the floor |
| Deploying a beacon | High open slope, not the valley floor |

## The 45-degree rule

As a rule of thumb, if you can see the point where the beacon is deployed (even if the beacon itself is hidden), the path usually works. If a ridge blocks the view, assume it blocks the radio too, and plan the search to cross ridges.
