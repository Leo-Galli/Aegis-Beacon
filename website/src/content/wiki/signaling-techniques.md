# Signaling Techniques

The beacon is the best signaling tool in your kit, but it is not the only one, and sometimes it is not the best one. The full toolbox, ranked by reliability in different situations.

## The toolbox

| Tool | Best for | Range | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beacon (radio) | Night, bad weather, over terrain | Up to km | Battery |
| Whistle | Near teams, forest | 500 m - 1 km | Nothing |
| Mirror | Clear sky, daytime | Up to 15 km | Sun |
| Fire / smoke | Day or night, open sky | km (visual) | Fuel, care |
| Ground signals | Aircraft, open ground | km (visual) | Space |

## The six-blast whistle

The universal distress whistle signal is six blasts in a row, pause, repeat. The reply is three blasts. It is the audible version of SOS and works on any whistle. One long blast is "attention" and is not a distress call.

## Combining signals

Use them in layers: beacon transmitting continuously, whistle when you hear searchers, mirror when you see or hear an aircraft. Layers cover each other's failure modes. A beacon in the snow plus a mirror flashing at the search helicopter is a textbook combination.

## Signal discipline

- Do not waste battery on beeping when you know searchers are close; the whistle works and preserves the beacon.
- Mirror flashes should be slow and deliberate; frantic flashing is unreadable.
- Fire smoke color matters: white smoke from green wood is standard; black smoke from plastic is toxic and confusing.
