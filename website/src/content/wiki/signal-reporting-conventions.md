# Signal Reporting Conventions

## Overview

When you test with another radio operator, a structured signal report makes results comparable. Amateur radio uses RST (readability, strength, tone); for the beacon's CW signal the same language applies.

## RST Explained

| Letter | Meaning | Scale |
|--------|---------|-------|
| R | Readability | 1-5 |
| S | Signal strength | 1-9 |
| T | Tone quality | 1-9 |

## For the Beacon's CW

| Component | What to judge |
|-----------|---------------|
| R | Can you copy the payload? |
| S | Meter or ear strength (1-9) |
| T | Clean keying vs chirpy/buzzy (1-9) |

A clean report for the beacon might be "599" - perfectly readable, strong, clean tone.

## Strength Words

| Plain-English term | Rough dBm |
|--------------------|-----------|
| Barely there | Below -110 |
| Weak | -100 to -90 |
| Fair | -90 to -80 |
| Good | -80 to -70 |
| Strong | -70 to -60 |
| Very strong | Above -60 |

## Reporting to Another Operator

Example exchange:

- "I'm copying your CW 599 at 433.500 MHz."
- "Signal 559, keying clean, no chirp."

This tells the beacon owner their radio, antenna and timing are working.
