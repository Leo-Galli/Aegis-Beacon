# Ridge Crossing Technique

The ridge is the universal radio obstacle, and the fix is embarrassingly simple: get to the crest. This page is the technique, with the physics that explains why it works.

## The crest rule

A signal does not need to see over a ridge; it needs to diffract over the edge. The diffraction point is the crest, and its effectiveness depends on the clearance of the [Fresnel Zone](fresnel-zone). The practical version:

- On the ridge: full signal.
- 20 m below the crest: often nothing.
- On the crest line, 100 m along it: full signal again.

The difference between dead and alive is measured in meters of height and meters of lateral position.

## The technique

1. Reach the ridge crest before attempting the transmission.
2. Walk along the crest until the beacon's bearing is roughly perpendicular to the ridge: that is the diffraction sweet spot.
3. Hold the receiver high (see [Antenna Height Matters](antenna-height-matters)).
4. Test, and if the signal is marginal, walk 10 - 20 m further along the crest and retest.

## The search application

For a searcher with a receiver, the ridge is not the end of the search: it is the observation post. Check the crest before descending into the next valley, and use the [Homing Technique](homing-technique) from the crest to refine the bearing.

## The antenna polarization note

At the crest, the diffracted signal can be depolarized. Tilting the receiver antenna 45 degrees sometimes finds 5 - 10 dB the vertical position missed.
