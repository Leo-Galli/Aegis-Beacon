# Battery Storage

Most cells are killed by storage, not by use. The three storage mistakes: stored full, stored empty, stored hot. This page is the correct procedure.

## The storage voltage

| State | Voltage | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stored full | 4.2 V | Fastest aging; calendar capacity loss |
| Stored empty | below 2.8 V | Cell damage, possible death |
| Storage charge | 3.6 - 3.8 V (40 - 60%) | Minimal aging |

The rule: store at 3.7 V, roughly 50%. A beacon coming out of storage at 50% is also ready to use faster.

## Temperature

- Ideal storage: 10 - 20 C, dry.
- Never store in a hot car or on a radiator: heat accelerates every degradation process.
- Cold storage is fine (the chemistry tolerates it) but condensation is not: dry, sealed storage wins.

## The calendar

| Interval | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Every 3 months | Measure the voltage; top up to 3.7 V if below 3.6 V |
| Every year | Run a capacity check (see [Battery Troubleshooting](battery-troubleshooting)) |
| Every 2 years | Replace cells that fail the capacity check |

## The long-term storage decision

For a beacon stored for a season: leave the cell in place at 50% or remove it? Removing it protects the device's springs and the cell's contacts, but a beacon with no cell cannot be picked up and used. The compromise: cell installed, at 50%, checked quarterly.
