# Austria Radio Legal

Austria's regulator RTR applies the CEPT harmonized framework. The beacon's 433 MHz ISM operation is license-free within the standard limits, and Austrian mountain rescue (Bergrettung) is among the most active users of coordination radio in the Alps.

## ISM operation

The 433.050 - 434.790 MHz band is license-free for short-range devices under the harmonized SRD rules, with the standard 10 mW ERP ceiling for general use. CE-marked modules satisfy the equipment side.

## The power ceiling and duty cycle

As across CEPT: routine operation at or below the ERP ceiling, duty-cycle limits for continuous use, and higher settings reserved for genuine emergencies. The [Duty Cycle Rule](duty-cycle-rule) and [ERP vs EIRP](erp-vs-eirp) pages give the full reasoning.

## Bergrettung context

Austrian mountain rescue operates its own coordination channels, which the beacon does not transmit on and must not interfere with. If a rescue is in progress near you, the practical rule is: listen first (SEARCH mode costs nothing), transmit only if you are the one in need. The [Rescue Communication Basics](rescue-communication-basics) page covers the etiquette.

## The emergency exception

Austrian law, like the rest of the CEPT framework, recognizes distress: a person in danger may use whatever means are necessary to summon help. EMERGENCY mode is the beacon's implementation of this principle.

## Amateur alternative

Austrian amateur licensing (OE callsigns) opens the 70 cm band with full privileges and mandatory identification (see [Callsign Identification](callsign-identification)).
