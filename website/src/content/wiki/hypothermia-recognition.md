# Hypothermia Recognition

Hypothermia kills more mountain users than any other single cause, and it is insidious: the victim loses judgment first, which is exactly the loss that prevents self-rescue. Recognition is a skill, not a feeling.

## The stages

| Stage | Body temperature | Signs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mild | 35 - 32 C | Shivering, clumsiness, apathy |
| Moderate | 32 - 28 C | Shivering stops, confusion, stumbling, mumbling |
| Severe | below 28 C | Unconsciousness, no pulse perception |

## The key sign: behavioral change

The most reliable early indicator is not shivering: it is a change in behavior. A talkative partner goes quiet. A careful person starts dropping things. Someone who was leading is suddenly following. These changes come before the victim can articulate them.

## The "umbles"

Classic mnemonic: the victim stumbles, mumbles, fumbles, and grumbles. When you see the umbles, act: shelter, insulation, food, warmth, and consider evacuation. This is the point where the beacon starts earning its place, because the window for self-help is closing.

## What to do

1. Get the victim out of wind and wet immediately.
2. Add insulation layers, including under the head.
3. Give warm sweet drinks and food; no alcohol, no caffeine.
4. Do not rub limbs; that moves cold blood to the core.
5. Evacuate. A hypothermic person who stops shivering is in the dangerous phase, not the recovered phase.

## The beacon's role

If hypothermia is present, the emergency plan starts earlier than you would like: warm shelter first, then transmit position while the team organizes evacuation. The beacon's repeating bursts let the rescuers track a deteriorating situation without further transmissions from the victim.
