# Storm Operations

Storms are when the beacon earns its keep, and also when everything about it gets harder. This page is the storm operating manual.

## What the storm does

| Element | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Rain on the case | Water paths open (see [Waterproofing](waterproofing-and-enclosure-sealing)) |
| Wet antenna | Loss and detuning (see [Snow and Signal Behavior](snow-and-signal-behavior)) |
| Cold | Battery sag (see [Cold Weather Batteries](cold-weather-batteries)) |
| Wind | Antenna flexing, case abrasion |
| Lightning | The radio must be OFF or the antenna DOWN during active lightning |

## The tactics

1. **Protect the seals**: check the case before the storm, not during.
2. **The antenna wipe**: before each important burst, wipe the antenna and connector.
3. **Battery first**: the cold sags the cell; warm the case before TX (see [Winter Pack](winter-pack)).
4. **The ridge rule**: storms make ridges dangerous; move off the crest to a lee position, and use the [Ridge Crossing Technique](ridge-crossing-technique) understanding in reverse: the lee position still needs a path.

## Lightning discipline

The beacon's antenna is a lightning attractor. During active lightning: antenna down or the device off. The beacon's transmitting schedule matters less than not being the tallest conductor on the slope. Resume beaconing when the storm passes overhead.

## The plan change

A storm changes the emergency plan: visibility drops, aircraft cannot fly, and the [Incident Command](incident-command) system switches to ground-based search. The beacon's repeating bursts become the only continuous signal the teams have: battery management becomes the mission.
