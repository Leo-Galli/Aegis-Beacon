# Snow Shelter Signaling

A snow shelter (igloo, snow cave, dugout) is the correct response to a storm or a night out. It is also a radio problem: the signal must escape the snow around you.

## How snow affects the signal

Wet snow absorbs 433 MHz strongly; dry, fresh snow less so. A beacon inside a shelter with a meter of wet snow above it can lose 20+ dB (see [Snow and Signal Behavior](snow-and-signal-behavior)). The signal does not die entirely: it escapes through the entrance and any thinner walls.

## The antenna strategy

| Placement | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Buried in the snow wall | Worst: full absorption |
| At the entrance, pointing out | Best: the path is open air |
| On a pole through the roof vent | Good: above the snow |
| Against the wall, high | Acceptable |

The rule: the antenna wants the least snow between it and the sky, and the entrance is the natural escape hatch.

## The survival sequence

1. Shelter first: the beacon runs for hours, the person does not (see [Hypothermia Recognition](hypothermia-recognition)).
2. Then deploy the antenna at the entrance.
3. Then confirm: one burst decoded by a receiver at the surface (see [Pack Check Routine](pack-check-routine)).
4. Then keep the schedule: the repeating bursts are the status report.

## The marker

A visual marker at the entrance (a pole, a colored item) complements the radio: searchers homing on the beacon see the marker for the final meters (see [Homing Technique](homing-technique)).
