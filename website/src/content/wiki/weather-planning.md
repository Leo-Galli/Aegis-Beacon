# Weather Planning

Mountain weather is the variable that turns a routine trip into an incident. This page is the checklist: forecast reading, decision points, and what weather does to the beacon.

## Reading the forecast

- Check the forecast for the altitude of your route, not the valley town.
- Look at wind, temperature, precipitation, and cloud base separately; the "sunny" headline hides all of them.
- Mountain forecasts change faster than valley ones; recheck the morning of the trip.

## The decision points

| Sign | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Thunderstorms forecast for the afternoon | Start earlier, or go another day |
| Wind above 50 km/h on the ridge | Reconsider the ridge crossing |
| Rain at the summit altitude | Plan for hypothermia risk (see [Hypothermia Recognition](hypothermia-recognition)) |
| Snow level dropping toward your route | Turn around before it reaches you |

## What weather does to the beacon

| Weather | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Rain and wet snow | Antenna and connector losses rise; water in connectors |
| Cold | Battery capacity drops (see [Cold Weather Batteries](cold-weather-batteries)) |
| Storm | Noise on the band, but the beacon still transmits |
| Dense fog | No RF effect, but search aircraft cannot see you |

## The beacon-specific preparation

- Wet weather: check the case seals and the antenna mount before the trip (see [Waterproofing](waterproofing-and-enclosure-sealing)).
- Cold: keep the spare cell inside your jacket (see [Cold Weather Batteries](cold-weather-batteries)).
- Storm: the beacon keeps working; it is the flying rescue that waits.
