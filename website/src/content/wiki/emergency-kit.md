# Emergency Kit

The beacon is the signal; the emergency kit is everything the signal needs to matter. This page is the beacon-adjacent pouch: small, checked with the beacon, always packed together.

## The pouch contents

| Item | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Spare 18650 cell | The beacon's runtime extension (see [Battery Field Swap](battery-field-swap)) |
| Whistle | The close-range signal (see [Whistle Signals](whistle-signals)) |
| Signal mirror | The daylight long-range signal (see [Mirror Signaling](mirror-signaling)) |
| Thermal blanket | Hypothermia defense (see [Hypothermia Recognition](hypothermia-recognition)) |
| Notepad and pencil | Writing coordinates, logging (see [Beacon Log Template](beacon-log-template)) |
| Small first aid kit | The basics (see [First Aid Kit](first-aid-kit)) |
| Spare antenna and SMA tool | The most common field failure (see [Antenna Mounting](antenna-mounting)) |
| Emergency contact card | Frequencies, callsign, contacts (see [Emergency Communications Plan](emergency-communications-plan)) |

## The one-pouch rule

Keep these items together with the beacon, not scattered across the pack. When you reach for the beacon in a crisis, the pouch comes with it: the signal, the tools to maintain it, and the tools that work alongside it.

## The check

The [Pack Check Routine](pack-check-routine) checks the pouch as part of the five-minute routine: cell charged, mirror present, card current, blanket sealed.

## The card

The emergency contact card is the paper version of the beacon's payload: your callsign, your frequency, your contacts, your emergency plan. It costs nothing, needs no battery, and survives everything except being left behind.
